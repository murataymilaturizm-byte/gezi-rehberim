# How to Sell Tours Online: A Roadmap for New Agencies in Türkiye (2026)

The centre of gravity of tour sales moved from the shop window to the screen years ago: today's customer discovers the tour on Instagram, verifies it on Google, asks about it on WhatsApp — and often buys without ever meeting you face to face. For a new agency this is both an opportunity and a trap. An opportunity, because a small agency with the right setup can sell nationwide; a trap, because "being on the internet" and "selling on the internet" are not the same thing.

This guide is an end-to-end roadmap for agencies that have obtained their operating licence ([our founding guide](/en/blog/seyahat-acentesi-nasil-acilir) covers those steps) and want to start selling: which channels exist and where to start, the legal obligations, how to collect money, how to plan the first 30 days, and what raises conversion. A 12-question FAQ closes the guide.

> **In short:** Online tour sales stand on three channels: social media + ads for **discovery**, Google visibility and a website for **trust**, and WhatsApp for **the sale itself**. On the legal side, distance-selling and pre-information obligations apply; on collection, you start with bank transfer and mature into a virtual POS. The link that breaks most often is failing to keep up with the message traffic ads generate — build your answering capacity before you open the channel.

## The Channel Map: Where Tours Get Discovered, Where They Get Sold

Thinking of online sales as a single "place" is the first misconception of new agencies. In reality the customer journey passes through three stations, each with its own channel:

| Station | Channel | Its job |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Discovery** | Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, targeted ads | The customer sees your tour for the first time |
| **Verification** | Google Business Profile, website, reviews | "Is this agency real and trustworthy?" |
| **Sale** | WhatsApp (the de facto sales channel in Türkiye) | Q&A, dates, deposit, booking |

The critical property of this trio is that it is a chain: the customer who sees the ad checks your profile, goes from the profile to Google, and — if convinced — writes on WhatsApp. **The weakest link's rate is the whole chain's sales rate** — a perfect ad's traffic evaporates on an unanswered WhatsApp line.

### What about OTAs (ready-made marketplaces)?
Tour and activity marketplaces offer ready traffic and can be a visibility shortcut for a new agency. The price is commission and the fact that **the customer belongs to the platform**: the number doesn't stay with you, so there is no repeat sale. The balanced strategy: use OTAs as an extra shop window, but put the real investment into the channel where the customer reaches **you directly**.

## The Legal Frame: What to Know Before Selling

There is no separate "e-sales licence" for selling tours online — your operating licence covers the channels. But remote selling activates additional consumer-law obligations in Türkiye:

- **Pre-information:** Before the sale, the scope of the tour, what the price includes, and the payment and cancellation-refund terms must be presented clearly and provably (written/digital).
- **Distance-selling regime:** In sales concluded over the internet, delivering the contract terms to the customer on a durable medium is essential. The package-tour rules on written contracts and information remain valid even when the channel is the internet.
- **Price and promise consistency:** The price and terms shown in the ad must match what is applied in the sale; "one price in the ad, another on WhatsApp" is both a trust and a legal problem.
- **KVKK (Turkish data-protection law):** Since you process customer data (names, phones, traveller details), you need a privacy notice and a data-retention routine.

Practical advice: have the tour contract + pre-information form + cancellation-refund policy prepared once as a **template set** and use the same set in every sale. That set is the agency's strongest defence on dispute day — and its absence is the most common way to lose — for this and similar traps see [The 10 Mistakes New Travel Agencies Make](/en/blog/acente-acarken-yapilan-10-hata).

## Collecting Payment: From Bank Transfer to Virtual POS

A new agency's collection ladder usually has three rungs:

1. **Bank transfer + receipt confirmation (day one):** Zero setup cost, and the customer habit in Türkiye is strong. The discipline requirement: state the deposit amount and the transfer-description format clearly, and confirm the booking **in writing** when the receipt arrives. The weakness: the customer must switch to their banking app — friction that produces abandonment in night-time sales.
2. **Virtual POS / payment link (first months):** Card instalments and "click-the-link-and-pay" convenience visibly raise conversion. Banks may want commercial history for a virtual POS; licensed payment institutions are a faster start for new businesses. Read the commission as a conversion investment, not a cost.
3. **Automated flow (maturity):** Booking record and payment instruction proceed in one flow — the deposit details go out automatically as the customer books, and payment status matches the record. At this point collection becomes an invisible step inside the sale.

Whichever rung you are on, two rules never change: **don't give firm bookings without a deposit** (it inflates cancellations and quota chaos), and **tie every collection to a record the same day** (who paid, how much, for which tour — mid-season memory is not enough).

## The First 30 Days: A Week-by-Week Plan

**Week 1 — Infrastructure:** Google Business Profile (photos + category + service area), a basic website (tours, prices, contact, cancellation policy), the WhatsApp line set up to business standard ([step-by-step setup guide](/en/blog/acenteler-icin-whatsapp-business-kurulumu)), the contract-form template set, and a clear price-quota table for 3-5 opening tours.

**Week 2 — Content and shop window:** A sales page/catalogue card per tour (cover image + programme + price + FAQ), social profiles completed, the first 9-12 posts (tour shots, destination facts, "questions we get asked"), a one-click WhatsApp link on every profile.

**Week 3 — First traffic:** A small-budget targeted ad (one tour, one audience — don't scatter), the click landing **directly in a WhatsApp chat** (don't put a form in between), and the network announcement ("early booking is open for our X tour" — a product announcement sells better than a founding announcement).

**Week 4 — Measure and correct:** How many wrote, how many asked the price, how many became bookings, and at which step did the drop-offs happen? The first month's data is small but directional: many messages and few sales points to answer quality/speed; many clicks and few messages points to ad-promise mismatch. Fix it and grow month two.

## The 5 Levers That Raise Conversion

1. **Speed:** The number-one determinant of online sales. The customer writing the same question to 3-4 agencies goes with the first satisfying answer. Minutes during working hours, automation outside them — the agency that leaves the overnight "any room for 4 tomorrow?" message until morning has usually left the sale too. For which automation fits which volume, see [Chatbot or Booking Assistant?](/en/blog/web-chatbot-mu-whatsapp-rezervasyon-asistani-mi).
2. **Clarity:** Price, included/excluded list, departure point and time, deposit-refund terms — visible without being asked. Every extra question a customer must ask is an opportunity to give up.
3. **Trust signals:** TÜRSAB licence number displayed, real tour photos (not stock), Google reviews, a clear refund policy. Transparency covers the new agency's review gap.
4. **Frictionless payment:** The difference between "let me send you an IBAN" and "you can pay at this link" is clearest in night-time sales.
5. **Follow-up:** The customer who asked but didn't buy is not lost — one polite reminder ("quota is tightening; still considering?") and seasonal re-contact are the cheapest second sales channel. Sustaining that requires records: who asked, for which tour, when.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**1. Does selling tours online require a separate licence/permit?**
No; your travel-agency operating licence covers the sales channels. What's additional is not a licence but obligations: pre-information, contracts and consumer-law compliance.

**2. Can I sell online without a website?**
Technically yes — the social media + WhatsApp pair produces sales. But the site is the verification station: an agency that can't be found on Google fails many customers' trust threshold. A simple but clear site is a minimum, not a luxury.

**3. How much ad budget should I allocate?**
Think of month one as a "learning budget": one tour, a narrow audience, a small amount; measure and scale. What decides is not the amount but the preparation — a big budget opened before answering capacity exists produces unanswered messages.

**4. Why don't ad customers fill in forms?**
Because in the messaging age a form is friction. Route the click straight into a WhatsApp chat; asking a question is always easier than filling a form.

**5. What should the deposit rate be?**
20-30% is common in the sector. Zero deposit filters nothing and inflates non-serious bookings; an excessive deposit filters out serious customers; the middle protects your quota.

**6. The customer wants card instalments instead of a transfer; what now?**
That's the signal to move to the virtual-POS/payment-link rung. Instalments are a strong conversion lever in tour sales; weigh the commission against the price of the sales you lose without it.

**7. Does selling on OTAs kill my own channel?**
It doesn't kill it, but it doesn't feed it either. OTAs give visibility, not customer data. The healthy balance: keep OTAs as an extra shop window and win the customer over to your direct channel (WhatsApp/site) at every opportunity.

**8. How do I keep up with messages arriving at night?**
With a system, not a night shift: it starts with an after-hours auto-reply and evolves — as volume grows — into a WhatsApp AI assistant that completes the booking end to end: dates, party size and contact details are collected, the record is created; you see it in the panel in the morning.

**9. Should I publish prices openly on the site/social media?**
Yes. The "DM for price" routine produces messages short-term but grows low-intent traffic and erodes trust. Open pricing attracts serious customers and frees you from the price-haggling message pile.

**10. How do I manage cancellation-refund requests in online sales?**
A written policy from the start + sharing it at the moment of sale + record-keeping. When a request comes, the policy is applied as written; exceptions are given in writing, not verbally. Ambiguity is the mother of the internet's most expensive reviews.

**11. Most "how much?" messages from social media never convert; is that normal?**
It is — a discovery channel also produces low-intent questions. What you should measure is absolute sales, not the ratio; and fast, clear answers extract sales even from the "just asking" crowd. Carrying the answer load with automation turns that traffic from a cost into an opportunity.

**12. What is the single most critical metric until my first sale?**
Your message-response time. Traffic, content and ads can all be tuned; a late reply resets everything. If you watch one metric in the first 30 days, watch that one.
