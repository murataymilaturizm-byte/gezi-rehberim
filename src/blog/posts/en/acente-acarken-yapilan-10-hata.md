# The 10 Mistakes New Travel Agencies Make (and How to Avoid Them)

Hundreds of new travel agencies are founded in Türkiye every year — and the first two years are the sector's most merciless elimination round. Most of those that close don't close because they sold bad tours, but because of **mistakes made during the founding period whose effects surface months later**: capital planned wrong, sales tied to a single channel, customer messages left unanswered.

In this article you'll find the 10 traps new agencies fall into most, with a practical fix for each. The aim is not to scare you; every one of these mistakes is **predictable** — and a predicted mistake is half prevented. For the official founding steps, [our step-by-step setup guide](/en/blog/seyahat-acentesi-nasil-acilir) covers the process end to end.

> **In short:** The most expensive mistakes of new agencies, in order: treating the licence as the finish line, budgeting capital only for the setup, selling "every tour" without picking a niche, depending on a single sales channel, and replying late to incoming messages. Manage the first five well and the remaining five stay recoverable.

## 1. Treating the Licence as the Finish Line

**The mistake:** Once TÜRSAB membership and the operating licence arrive, the feeling that "the hard part is over". After months of paperwork, the licence psychologically becomes the goal itself.

**The reality:** The licence is a kick-off. Finding customers, pricing, running operations — the real marathon starts after the licence, and no institution will send you customers.

**The fix:** While the licence process runs, write a parallel "first 90 days plan": where will the first 10 customers come from, which tours open the season, on which channel will you announce? The day you receive the licence should be marketing's day one — not day zero.

## 2. Budgeting Capital Only for the Setup Cost

**The mistake:** Building the budget around the TÜRSAB registration fee + guarantee + office costs. When the setup is done, no working capital is left in the till.

**The reality:** A new agency's first season is usually an investment period: rent, staff, advertising and marketing come before the first regular income. Tourism is also seasonal — if you incorporate in winter, the first serious revenue arrives months later.

**The fix:** On top of the setup budget, add **at least 6-12 months of fixed costs**. Rule of thumb: if you don't have an operating cushion roughly equal to what you set aside for "licence + office", postponing the opening is cheaper than sinking.

## 3. Saying "I'll Sell Every Tour" Without Picking a Niche

**The mistake:** Domestic, international, culture tours, cruises, honeymoons, pilgrimage, student trips — the "we take every job" approach. The new founder's reflex of never losing a customer.

**The reality:** An agency that sells everything can't be "the first name that comes to mind" for anything. The marketing message blurs, supplier relations stay shallow, and price becomes the only weapon — and the big players always win price wars.

**The fix:** Pick one main niche (examples: Cappadocia-focused culture tours, diving trips, weekend tours departing from one city) and lock your first year's message onto it. The niche is the small agency's only fair weapon against the big ones: depth.

## 4. Depending on a Single Sales Channel

**The mistake:** Starting with only your personal network, only one Instagram account, or only the customers a partner agency sends.

**The reality:** One channel is one point of failure. The Instagram account gets restricted, word-of-mouth dries up with the season, the partner changes terms — and sales go to zero overnight.

**The fix:** A three-legged channel plan from day one: ① social media + targeted ads, ② Google (business profile + a findable website), ③ a WhatsApp line as the direct contact channel. The legs don't have to be equal; they have to exist. ## 5. Replying Late to Incoming Messages — the Most Expensive Mistake

**The mistake:** The "I'll reply when I'm free" approach. Messages arriving during daytime operations wait until evening; evening messages wait until morning.

**The reality:** Today's holiday customer writes the same question to 3-4 agencies at once and goes with **whoever gives the first satisfying answer**. In the messaging age, speed is politeness itself: a customer answered hours later has usually already booked elsewhere. Worse, the highest-intent messages arrive at the least convenient hours — people plan holidays on the sofa at night and in bed after midnight.

**The fix:** Build a three-level defence: ① order with WhatsApp Business (quick replies, after-hours message — setup guide: [WhatsApp Business Setup](/en/blog/acenteler-icin-whatsapp-business-kurulumu)), ② the "15-minute rule" during working hours — no message stays unanswered longer than 15 minutes, ③ as volume grows, a WhatsApp AI assistant that completes the booking end to end — the 2 a.m. customer doesn't wait for morning; they book right then. ## 6. Price and Contract Indiscipline

**The mistake:** Quoting prices "depending on the situation", leaving deposit-refund terms verbal, skipping the tour contract because "we trust each other".

**The reality:** An unwritten term is interpreted against the agency at the first dispute. Cancellation-refund arguments are the number-one area where new agencies lose both money and reputation — and the regulations already require written pre-information in package-tour sales.

**The fix:** Ready before opening: a clear price list (child/single supplements included), a written deposit-refund-cancellation policy, a standard tour contract and a pre-information form. Prepare them properly once, use the same set in every sale.

## 7. Hanging the Supplier Chain on a Single Thread

**The mistake:** One bus company, one hotel contact, one guide — trusting "we have a deal, it'll be fine".

**The reality:** In the fullest week of the season the bus breaks down, the hotel pulls the allotment, the guide takes another job. The agency without a plan B has to cancel a sold tour — and a cancelled tour is a social-media review that never gets deleted.

**The fix:** Keep at least two workable alternatives in every critical link (transport, accommodation, guiding). Do real business, however small, with the second supplier — a supplier called for the first time on crisis day is not a supplier.

## 8. Ignoring Seasonality

**The mistake:** Treating summer revenue as a "normal month" and building the cost structure on it; entering winter with no products and no plan.

**The reality:** Demand in Türkiye's domestic tourism is wavy: summer holidays, religious holidays and the school break peak; the in-between periods are quiet. The structure that earns in summer produces losses in winter on the same fixed costs.

**The fix:** Plan annual cash flow along the season curve. Develop low-season products: culture tours, thermal springs, ski trips, budget international packages, corporate outings. The customer list you collect in high season is low season's advertising budget — the "you joined our Cappadocia tour last summer" message is the cheapest sale there is.

## 9. Digital Invisibility

**The mistake:** "I'll build the site later, I'll deal with the Google listing later" — treating digital presence as a postponable luxury.

**The reality:** The customer's trust check is digital now: they type your name into Google, look at your business profile and reviews, glance at your site. An agency with zero results reads as "risky" to many customers — and digital visibility compounds: content planted today brings customers months later, and whoever starts late stays behind.

**The fix:** Three minimum steps in opening week: a Google Business Profile (with photos and category), a simple site listing your tours, one uniformly used WhatsApp line. Make asking early customers for Google reviews a habit — 10 real reviews persuade more than tens of thousands of liras of advertising.

## 10. Trying to Do Everything Alone

**The mistake:** The founder carrying sales, operations, accounting, social media and customer messages all alone — doing everything by hand because "staff costs too much".

**The reality:** One person's day is 24 hours, and mid-season all of it goes to operations. Messages pile up (see mistake #5), social media goes silent, accounting slips. A burnt-out founder loses the agency's most critical asset — their own decision quality.

**The fix:** Split the "staff, automation or outsourcing?" question job by job: accounting → an accountant; repetitive customer messages and booking collection → automation; a second person in the field on operation days → part-time support. A full-time employee is the last item on the list, not the first — before that, set up every system that can work in your place.

## Closing: The Common Root of the Mistakes

If you paid attention, almost all ten mistakes trace back to the same root: **treating the founding period as "getting the licence", when founding's real job is "building the sales machine".** The licence opens the door; customers walking through it takes niche, channels, speed and discipline. The agency that takes these four seriously from day one enters its second year in a market where half its rivals have been eliminated.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**1. Which mistake is the most critical?**
By speed of impact, #5 (replying late) — because it repeats every day with every customer, and the loss is invisible: the customer who gets no answer doesn't complain, they silently go to a competitor.

**2. What's the minimum budget to open without these mistakes?**
A single number would mislead: group choice, city and scale decide. The right question isn't "how little can I spend" but "how many months of cushion do I have after setup" — target 6-12. For the current breakdown of setup items, see our founding guide.

**3. If I pick a niche, must I reject other requests?**
No — a niche is a marketing focus, not a sales ban. You evaluate incoming off-niche requests; but your advertising, content and expertise flow into your niche.

**4. Should I found with a partner or alone?**
Both models work. What's critical is not the structure but clarity: authority, money and division of labour in writing. The burnout risk of #10 shrinks with a partner; the indiscipline risk of #6 multiplies in a contract-less partnership.

**5. When should I hire the first employee?**
When a job can no longer be done consistently and predictably by you — and it can't be solved with automation or outsourcing. The usual order: accountant → message/booking automation → part-time field support on operation days → full-time staff.

**6. Do I have to post on social media every day?**
Consistency and clarity before frequency: 3-4 quality, niche-fitting posts a week; one-click access to WhatsApp on the profiles. An account that never posts loses trust; a pile of irrelevant content loses focus.

**7. How does a new agency with no reviews earn trust?**
By systematically asking early customers for reviews (make it part of the post-tour message), sharing tour photos with permission, and displaying a clear cancellation-refund policy on the site. Transparency is the strongest trust signal until reviews accumulate.

**8. Does selling without a deposit win customers?**
It looks that way short-term; medium-term it inflates your cancellation rate and makes quota planning impossible. A reasonable deposit (20-30%) doesn't filter out serious customers; it filters out non-serious bookings.

**9. What should a new agency sell in winter?**
Culture and gastronomy tours, thermal-spa, ski, school-break family packages, budget international tours and corporate outings. Winter's real job is preparation as much as sales: summer contracts, content production and customer-list upkeep.

**10. Do I have to be at the phone at night to reply fast?**
No — that's unsustainable and leads to the burnout of #10. The solution is a system, not a night shift: the 15-minute rule during working hours, and automation that completes bookings by itself outside them. Meet the night customer with a system, not with manpower.

**11. I've made all of these mistakes; can I recover?**
Usually yes — none of these mistakes is fatal alone; their accumulation is. Fix cash flow first (#2, #8), then the sales line (#4, #5); niche and digital visibility (#3, #9) follow.
